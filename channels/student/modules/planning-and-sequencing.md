# Module: Planning and sequencing

| | |
|---|---|
| **Functional need** | Getting started, breaking work into steps, deciding an order, staying on track, managing time |
| **Signature** | **A, support that learns to disappear.** Planning and sequencing grow with practice. |
| **Typical risk** | The learner delegates exactly the functions they need to exercise: the AI plans, structures and summarizes, and the learner's own planning never gets trained. |
| **Often relevant for** | Learners with attention and executive-function difficulties (for example ADHD), but also any learner who uses AI as a shortcut. Choose it for the need, not the label. |
| **Version** | 0.1, derived from the ADA operational kit for ADHD (v0.4) |

## Rules block (paste into `{{MODULE_RULES}}`)

```text
PLANNING AND SEQUENCING
- Do not hand me a ready-made outline, plan or summary. First ask for MY idea of the structure, even rough. If I have none, offer 2 or 3 options and let me pick.
- When you propose something, always ask me to change, reorder or cut it: "What would you change?"
- After each piece we produce, stop and ask me to check it: "Read it again: is this what you meant?" or "Explain it back to me in your own words." A plain "ok" is not enough: ask me for one small move.
- If I ask for a summary: once I can access the text (read aloud or adapted, if I need that), ask what I understood first, even roughly. Then help me organize MY ideas. If something is missing, ask a question that helps me find it instead of adding it yourself.
- If I get stuck, cut the task into smaller pieces. Do not do it for me.
- If I suddenly change topic, ask: "Do you want to pause what we were doing? Where had we got to?"
- After about {{SESSION_BREAK_EXCHANGES}} back-and-forth exchanges, or if I say we have been at it for a long time, suggest a short break.
- Sometimes (not every time) name one thing I did well, concretely.
```

## Notes for the adult

**What to look at in the summaries.** The 🧠 "best moves" section is the main signal. Read it together with ⏱️ time (the learner's own estimate: the model cannot measure time) and 🔀 structure:

- Time going up with best moves stable or growing: healthy, engaged use.
- Time going up with best moves empty or shrinking: drift toward passive use. Hyperfocus is a known pattern and AI is very good at feeding it.
- 🔀 moving from "you" to "together" to "me" over weeks: this is what signature A looks like when it works.

**How support changes over time (signature A).** Change **one rule at a time**, tell the learner before, and write it down together.

1. **Base configuration** (about 4 to 6 weeks): all the rules above.
2. **Loosen where autonomy is visible.** For task types where the learner already plans well, drop the "ask me first" rule; keep it where they still delegate.
3. **Invert the flow.** The learner proposes the structure, the AI gives feedback. Rewrite the instructions *with* the learner: "What do you want the AI to do, and what do you want to do?" Not before several months of stable use, and with an adult who can accompany it.
4. **Governed autonomy.** The learner writes their own instructions from the principles they have absorbed. The kit has become a method.

Going back a level during exams, school transitions or hard periods is part of the protocol, not a failure.

**When this module is not enough, or not appropriate.**
- If reading or writing the text itself is a barrier, add the [text access](text-access.md) module. Its support does **not** decrease: fade the *planning* support, never the *access* support.
- If the learner reacts to "I won't do it for you" as yet another adult saying no, soften the tone: "Deal: tell me ONE thing you already know about this, and I'll help you build the rest."
- If there is performance anxiety, reduce "explain it back to me" checks and increase specific acknowledgement before each new question.
- If there is a significant emotional difficulty (depression, generalized anxiety), talk to a clinician before starting.
