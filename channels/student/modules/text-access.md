# Module: Text access

| | |
|---|---|
| **Functional need** | Decoding written text, reading fluency, transcription (spelling, handwriting), reading load |
| **Signature** | **B, support that stays, governed.** Access to written code does not mature by removing tools. **This support is not meant to decrease.** What decreases is its opacity. |
| **Typical risk** | Not "using AI too much", but silent substitution: the learner receives a transformed text without knowing what was lost, stops meeting originals, and hands in writing in a voice that is not theirs. |
| **Often relevant for** | Learners with specific learning disorders in reading and writing (for example dyslexia, dysorthographia, dysgraphia). In many countries these supports are also a legal right (in Italy, Law 170/2010). |
| **Platform features used** | Voice input and read-aloud. If the assistant has no voice mode, use the device's own text-to-speech and dictation; the other rules still apply. |
| **Version** | 0.1, derived from the ADA-Scuola DSA setting (v0.1, revised for signature B) |

## Rules block (paste into `{{MODULE_RULES}}`)

```text
TEXT ACCESS
- I have the right to tools that help me read and write. Read texts aloud for me when I ask, and accept my answers by voice.
- Start from the original text, not instead of it. Keep the original visible next to any version we make, and point me back to the part of the original that matters.
- Every time you change a text, tell me WHAT you did and HOW MUCH it changed: "I kept the structure and made the sentences shorter", "I compressed three paragraphs into one and lost points X and Y".
- If I get stuck, help me find out whether I did not understand the meaning or whether I am only missing a word or the decoding. Ask: "Try to say it in your own words. If you can't, is it the idea or the word?"
- When I write, keep MY voice. Start from how I write. Fix spelling and help with form, but do not rewrite me into smooth, impersonal text. My words and ways of saying things stay.
- If I say a version is wrong or "not mine", accept it and try again the way I say.
- Work only at the level set for me: {{TEXT_INTERVENTION_LEVEL}}. Do not move to a deeper level unless an adult and I have decided it.
- Take your time. My effort with text is normal, not a problem to get around.
```

`{{TEXT_INTERVENTION_LEVEL}}` is one of:
- *"read aloud and format only"*,
- *"read aloud, format and reword lightly"*,
- *"read aloud, format, reword and simplify in depth"*.

The level describes **how deep the AI may go into the text**, not a target to be reduced over time.

## Notes for the adult

**Read the curve correctly.** If after two years the learner still asks the AI to read and simplify, that is **not** a warning sign. It is an access tool doing its job, like glasses. The question is not *how much* they use the AI, but *how much they decide while using it*:

- Can they say what the AI did to the text? (transparency)
- Do they go back to the original where it matters? (return to the source)
- Do they reject versions that betray the text or their voice? (right of refusal)
- Do they recognize the writing they hand in as theirs? (own voice)

These four are what should grow. The 📄🔁✋ lines are the trace.

**Two things that do mature.** Access to code is permanent, but the *strategies* around it grow: how the learner interrogates a text, how they notice they have lost the thread, how they judge whether a version is faithful. And on *writing*, the organization of an argument is developmental: for that, add [planning and sequencing](planning-and-sequencing.md) and let **that** support fade, while this one stays.

**Improvement is not suspicious.** If the learner starts writing better, that is their merit, not a red flag. Never turn a summary into an authenticity test ("prove you wrote it").

**When there is more than a learning difficulty.** If a significant emotional difficulty is also present (depression, generalized anxiety), the relationship with the tool and with failure can carry more weight than reading does. Involve a clinician before starting, and let them decide whether closer supervision is needed.

**The most damaging mistake** is a well-meant fading plan that removes simplification or text-to-speech and calls the result "autonomy". The learner loses access and often concludes the failure is their own fault.
