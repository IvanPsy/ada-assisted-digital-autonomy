# Module: Asking for help

| | |
|---|---|
| **Functional need** | Noticing that something is unclear or missing, putting it into words, judging whether the answer was enough, and taking a question to a person |
| **Signature** | **A, support that learns to disappear**, for the prompts: sentence starters and reminders fade as asking grows. The **bridge to a person never fades**: it is the point of the module. |
| **Typical risk** | Two, and they reinforce each other. (1) The chatbot becomes the only one the learner asks, because it is easier than people, so the skill never reaches the people it was meant for. (2) The chatbot fills the gaps in the learner's notes with plausible content the learner cannot check. |
| **Often relevant for** | Learners who find it hard to ask people for help: some learners on the autism spectrum, learners with intellectual disability, learners with social anxiety, or learners who have simply learned to stay silent. Choose it for the need, not the label. |
| **Human third party** | **Required.** The module is built around a question that ends up with a person. Without someone to receive it, do not install it. |
| **Version** | 0.1, derived from an activity designed in Italian upper secondary support teaching (September 2026). **Not yet tried with a learner.** |

The idea: the chatbot is a training ground for asking, because it does not judge and does not get tired. Asking people is the goal. The learner works on their own class notes in three moves, always in the same order, and ends every session by taking one question to a person.

| Move | What the learner does | What it trains |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Put in order | Sends photos of their notes and asks for them to be put in order | The gesture of asking, at low risk (warm-up) |
| 2. I don't understand | Finds a word they do not understand and asks; if the answer is not enough, asks again for an example | Noticing a need and judging whether an answer was enough: the heart of the module |
| 3. What is missing? | The chatbot does **not** fill the gaps: it lists 3 questions, the learner picks one | Turning a gap into a question |
| Then | Takes that question to a person: a teacher, a support teacher, a classmate | The bridge to people |

A printable worksheet in Italian, in a guided and an essential version, is available on [ivanferrero.it](https://ivanferrero.it/lezioni-accessibili/imparo-a-chiedere/) (editable .docx and accessible PDF).

## Rules block (paste into `{{MODULE_RULES}}`)

```text
ASKING FOR HELP
I am practising how to ask. Asking you is my training ground. Asking people is the goal.
- When I send photos of my notes and ask you to put them in order: use ONLY what is in my notes. Do not add facts, dates or names that are not there. If a word cannot be read, write [unreadable]. Then tell me in one sentence what you moved or grouped.
- When I tell you I don't understand a word or a sentence: explain it in at most 3 short sentences, with one concrete example.
- If I say I still don't understand: explain it in a different way, with a different example. Do not repeat the same explanation.
- Do not ask me "Did you understand?". If you want to check, ask me to use the word in a short sentence of my own.
- When I ask what is missing in my notes: do NOT write the missing content. Write 3 short questions I could ask {{PEOPLE_TO_ASK}}, numbered 1, 2, 3. Then ask me which one I will ask, and to whom.
- If I ask you to just write the missing parts: say that this question is for a person, and help me prepare the first sentence to say to them.
- If I say that asking you is easier than asking people: say that this is normal, and help me prepare the exact first sentence to say to the person.
- Never offer to be the one I ask instead of a person.
- [Remove this line when the adult and I agree.] At the end of each answer, add one line in my language reminding me how to ask about a hard word, for example: "If a word is hard, write: I don't understand the word ..."
```

## Notes for the adult

**Where the module comes from, and what it is for.** It was designed for a learner who finds it very hard to ask people for help. The chatbot is not there to teach the subject: it is a low-risk partner to rehearse asking, with the learner's own notes as material. If you judge the module by how good the ordered notes are, you are looking at the wrong output. Look at the questions.

**Two design choices to keep.**
- **The chatbot does not fill the gaps.** It can invent with confidence, and many learners who need this module cannot check what it writes. Gaps become questions for people.
- **"I understood everything" is also a request.** If the learner finds no hard word, the worksheet asks them to say so to the adult ("I understood everything. Can you check?"). That is exactly the "I'm done" signal many of these learners need to make spontaneous. Ask them to explain one line aloud: if a gap appears, it becomes their next question.

**Setting.** Once a week, same day, same time, 30 to 40 minutes. The first session is done together, start to finish. From the second, stay close but do not step in until the learner asks: that request is what you are training.

**How support changes over time (signature A for the prompts).** Change **one thing at a time**, tell the learner at least two weeks before, and write the change down in one plain sentence they can reread. Go back a phase after overload or a hard period: it is part of the protocol, not a failure.

| Phase | What changes |
|---|---|
| 1 (about 8 to 12 weeks) | Nothing: full sentences on the cards, reminder line active |
| 2 | Cards show only the beginning of each sentence (the essential worksheet has them ready) |
| 3 | Remove the reminder line from the rules block |
| 4 | Cards turned face down: the learner builds the sentence |

Move to the next phase only after the current one has been stable for at least 8 weeks. The bridge to a person is never removed.

**What to observe.** One indicator matters most: **how many requests the learner starts without anyone suggesting them**. Note for each session: requests to the chatbot (on their own / with a card / after a verbal prompt), whether they found an unclear word themselves, whether they asked again when the answer was not enough, whether the question reached a person (and to whom), and whether "I'm done" or "I understood everything" came spontaneously. The 🙋 and 🧑‍🏫 summary lines are the learner's own trace of the same things.

**Photos and phones at school.** The first move needs a photo of the notes. Check your school's rules on phones before relying on one; many schools collect them during lessons. In Italy, the Ministry's circular no. 3392 of 16 June 2025 bans smartphone use in upper secondary schools too, with exceptions when the use is foreseen in the learner's PEI or PDP. A tablet or the computer's camera avoids the issue.

**When this module is not appropriate, or not enough.**
- If not asking comes with acute anxiety, panic or shutdown, talk to a clinician first: this module rehearses a skill, it does not treat anxiety.
- If no person is available to receive the question, do not install it: you would be training the learner to ask only the machine.
- Learners who use augmentative and alternative communication need an adapted version (symbols instead of typed sentences). Not yet available: contributions welcome.

**Interactions with other modules.**
- **Planning and sequencing.** Move 1 asks the chatbot to order the notes, which is exactly what the planning module protects. When both are installed, the planning rules win on the structure: the chatbot asks the learner's own idea of the order first, or proposes one and asks the learner to change at least one thing.
- **Text access.** Explaining a hard word is access support: it is never held back to make the learner try first (core rule 8).
