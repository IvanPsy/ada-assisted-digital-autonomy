# Template: Accessible text

For adapting a reading text (textbook page, article, instructions) so that a learner can access it **without losing contact with the original and without losing the decisions**. Built on signature B: the adaptation is allowed to stay forever, as long as it is transparent.

## Request to the co-pilot

```text
Please adapt this text using the accessible-text template.
Learner needs (from profile sheet): [e.g. reading load, decoding, long sentences]
Intervention level: [format only / light rewording / in-depth simplification]
Subject and goal of the lesson: [...]
Original text: [paste]
```

## Output structure

```markdown
### [Title of the text]

**Before you read:** [one sentence on what the text is about and why it matters for today's goal]

**Key words** (max 5)
| Word | Meaning in this text |
|---|---|

---

[Adapted text, formatted for access:
 - short paragraphs, one idea each
 - one sentence per line where helpful
 - subject-verb-object order, few subordinate clauses (only at "light rewording" level or deeper)
 - bold for the key concept of each paragraph, nothing else in bold]

---

**What I changed** (for the learner and the adult)
- Kept: [structure / order of ideas / all facts]
- Changed: [shortened sentences / replaced technical words with explanations]
- Compressed or left out: [e.g. "paragraphs 3-4 compressed into one; the dates of the second example are not here"]

**Back to the original**
1. [A question the learner can only answer by looking at a specific part of the original: "In the original, paragraph 2, find the word the author uses instead of 'fast'."]
2. [A question that asks the learner to decide: "Which of the three ideas above matters most for today's question? Why?"]
```

## Notes

- **Never deliver only the adapted version.** The original stays next to it, on paper or on screen.
- **The "What I changed" box is not optional.** It is what turns a silent substitution into a governed extension.
- **The decision question is not optional either.** Accessible material that makes every choice for the learner removes a barrier and an opportunity at the same time.
- For learners who use text-to-speech, check that the formatting reads well aloud (avoid tables for running text, avoid symbols read as noise).
