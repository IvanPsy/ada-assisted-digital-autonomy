# Template: Weekly check-in

A short, predictable conversation between the learner and one adult, built around the session summaries. It is the "human third party": the part of reflection that should not be delegated to the AI itself.

**Strongly recommended, with exemptions.** If another adult in the learner's network already does this (a clinician who regularly discusses AI use, a support teacher who reads summaries with the learner), this check-in can become fortnightly, monthly, or be dropped. What is not acceptable is that nobody does it.

## Setting

- **When:** once a week, same day and time.
- **How long:** 10 minutes, no more.
- **Where:** quiet, no screens on (including the adult's phone).
- **Tone:** descriptive, not evaluative. You are looking *together*, not judging.
- **Material:** one or two session summaries **chosen by the learner**, printed or copied onto paper so that screens can stay off.

## The four questions

Ask them in this order. Do not add more. You are not doing therapy.

1. **Time.** "Roughly how much time did you spend studying with the AI this week? More or less than last week?"
2. **A move of your own.** "Was there a moment when you worked something out yourself, with the AI's help but without it giving you the answer?"
3. **The shortcut.** "Was there a time you noticed you were looking for the shortcut? What did you do?"
4. **Next week.** "What would you like to try differently next week?"

**Variations by module**
- *Text access:* replace question 3 with "Was there a version of a text that didn't feel right, or didn't sound like you? What did you do with it?"
- *Predictable interaction / role-play:* replace question 3 with "Was there something you asked the AI that you would have liked to ask a person? Did you practice anything you then tried for real?"

## What to do with the answers

Keep one short page per week (date, four brief answers, one free line). It is your observation tool, not a report. If the learner asks to see it, show it.

If two or more warning signs persist over two weeks or more (for example: "best moves" empty, irritation when the AI is unavailable, AI use moving late into the evening, withdrawal from people), bring it to the support teacher or clinician. Read time with the signature in mind: growing time on planning support (A) with empty "best moves" is a warning; steady use of reading support (B) is not. It is not an emergency; it is an observation worth sharing with someone competent to read it.

## What not to do

- Do not ask to read the conversations. The summaries are enough.
- Do not moralize ("you use it too much").
- Do not skip it when things go well: the regularity *is* the tool.
- Do not do it when you are tired or irritated. Postpone by a day.
