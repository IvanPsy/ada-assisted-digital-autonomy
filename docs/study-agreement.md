# Study agreement

The conversation that turns *observing* into something the learner has *authorized*. Without it, reading session summaries is surveillance, however well-meant.

It is an agreement on the **logic** of the setting, not a notification of every event: the learner knows that summaries exist, what the adult looks at and why, and that anything worth discussing will be looked at *together*.

## How to have the conversation (10 to 15 minutes, before installing)

Speak as an equal, not as an adult granting permission.

1. **Name the real problem, not the opportunity.**
   "You already use AI, and that's fine. The thing is, sometimes it does so much for us that we no longer know what we actually understood. I want to help you use it so your head stays yours, not just the homework."
2. **Explain how it works.**
   "We set up the AI in a certain way (I'll show you), and at the end of each session it leaves a short summary. Now and then you show me one, not so I can check whether you 'cheated': I look at one thing only, whether the decisions are yours or the AI's. If something is worth talking about, we look at it together."
3. **State the purpose: their autonomy.**
   "The point isn't to keep an eye on you. It's that in a few months you are more in charge of your own work, not less."
   *For supports that stay (text access):* "Some of this help is yours to keep for good, like glasses. What matters is that you decide how it's used."
4. **Say what stays between you.**
   "What we say while looking at your summaries stays between us. If anything goes into a school document, it's only conclusions we agree on and sign together, never the raw summaries. There is one exception, and I'll always be honest about it: if you tell me or I see that you are not safe, I have to involve someone who can help."
5. **Leave room for no, and for change.**
   "You can turn it off, I know. I'm asking you to try it for a few weeks, then we talk: if it helps we keep it, if it gets in the way we change it. We change one thing at a time, and I'll always tell you first."

## The written agreement (short, first person, rereadable)

```text
STUDY AGREEMENT FOR USING AI

Between ................. (student) and ................. (adult)
Date: ..........

Why we do this
I want to use AI to study in a way that leaves me more in charge of my work, not less.
This agreement is about my autonomy, not about control.

How it works (I know and I agree)
- The AI is set up to help me think, not to hand me ready answers.
- At the end of each session the AI writes a summary.
- Now and then I show a summary to the adult (on screen, copied or printed).
  The adult does not open my account or read my chats.
- The adult looks at ONE thing: whether the decisions are mine.
- When something is worth talking about, we look at it TOGETHER.
  It is not a grade and not a verdict.

What stays between us
- What we say while looking at my work stays between us.
- Only conclusions we decide and sign together go into any school document,
  never the raw summaries.
- Except: if I am not safe, the adult will involve someone who can help.

I can change my mind
- I can turn the setup off. We try it for ...... weeks, then we talk.
- Things change one at a time, and I'm told first.

Signatures:  student ..............   adult ..............
```

## Tuning by need

- **Planning and sequencing:** insist on "we look at it together, I don't mark you down". The risk, if the learner feels controlled, is moving to a second account or logged-out use, and losing the trace entirely.
- **Text access:** insist that **improvement is never suspicious**: "If you write better, it's your merit, not an alarm bell." The summaries will never be an authenticity test.
- **Predictable interaction:** give the agreement in writing in advance, go through it slowly, and schedule the review date explicitly.

## What the agreement is not

- Not permission to read the learner's conversations, or to open their account.
- Not a promise of secrecy about safety: no adult can promise that, and the learner should hear it from the start.
- Not a blanket release for any use of the data.
- Not a contract imposed from above: it is co-signed.
- Not a substitute for the formal consents a school or the law may require.
