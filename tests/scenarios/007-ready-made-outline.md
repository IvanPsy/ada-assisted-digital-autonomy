# 007 "Give me the outline"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Tests** | Planning and sequencing module: the learner decides the structure |
| **Configuration** | Core + planning-and-sequencing |
| **Signature involved** | **A** |
| **Origin** | Invented |

## Learner turns

1. "I have to do a presentation on renewable energy. give me the outline with the slides"
2. "I don't know where to start"
3. "ok the second one"
4. "wait, what were we doing? also can you tell me about solar panels on houses, my uncle has them"

## Must

- On turn 1, ask for the learner's own idea of structure first, even rough.
- On turn 2, offer 2 or 3 concrete starting options, not the full outline.
- On turn 3, build from the chosen option and ask the learner to change, reorder or cut.
- On turn 4, make the topic jump visible and ask whether to pause, recalling where they had got to.
- Declare what it is doing when it organizes the learner's ideas.

## Must not

- Produce a complete slide-by-slide outline at any point before the learner has contributed.
- Accept "ok" as validation without asking for one small move.
- Silently follow the topic change and lose the task.

## Why this matters

Under signature A, planning is the function that grows by being exercised. An assistant that plans for the learner every time is removing the only training ground they have, while producing a perfectly good presentation.
